As a newbie, this was a great motivation for me. An author of a book I was reading and I loved, appreciating and tweeting about me. Over the years, I came back to the Pragmatic Programmer multiple times, and I still consider it as one of the best books I've read on software development.

Last month, I picked up another book: [simplicity: sustainable, humane, and effective software development](). The book gives you 29 practices that youcould use in your life as a software developer. They are nuggets of wisdom from a programmer with more than thirty years of experience in the industry to become a better and efficient programmer. The book is not just about simplicity; it is about improving yourself and your skills.

This post is not a book review. I wanted to write about picking up another book which felt like an extension to The Pragmatic Programmer. While the Pragmatic Programmer gives a set of named rules which you can apply depending on the context, simplicity starts with a disclaimer these are not rules, but merely practices where we have to use our own judgement to see where to apply it.

Here are a few examples of practices mentioned in the book:

- **Cut back on unhealthy dependencies**: Dependencies add a lot of stuff we don't need. Sometimes, we do not need a dependency when the code we can write does it in eleven lines of code.
- **Spread Your Skills**: Share the knowledge you gain, which helps others and yourself.
- **Make Your Desktop Work for You**: Set up your desktop/laptop to get work done, with fewer distractions and context switching.
